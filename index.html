Ruling on hanging amulets containing Quranic verses.

by Sheikh Abdul-Azeez ibn Baaz • 5 min read • Fatawa

Question

Is writing amulets from Quranic verses and other texts and hanging them around the neck considered polytheism or not?

Answer :

It has been authentically reported from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said: “Incantations, amulets, and love charms are polytheism.” This was narrated by Ahmad, Abu Dawud, Ibn Majah, Ibn Hibban, and Al-Hakim, who authenticated it. Ahmad also narrated, as did Abu Ya'la and Al-Hakim, who authenticated it, on the authority of Uqbah ibn Amir ( may God be pleased with him), that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “Whoever wears an amulet, may God not grant him his wish, and whoever wears a seashell, may God not grant him peace.” Ahmad also narrated it from another chain of transmission on the authority of Uqbah ibn Amir with the wording: “ Whoever wears an amulet has committed polytheism .” There are many hadiths with this meaning. An amulet is something hung on children or other people to ward off the evil eye, jinn, illness, and the like. Some people call it a charm, and others call it a “comprehensive amulet.” There are two types:

The first type: is that which consists of the names of devils, bones, beads, nails, or talismans, which are isolated letters or similar things. This type is undoubtedly forbidden due to the abundance of evidence indicating its prohibition. It is a type of minor polytheism based on these hadiths and others with similar meaning. It may also be major polytheism if the one wearing the amulet believes that it will protect him or ward off harm. You can cause illness or ward off harm without God's permission and will.

The second type: what is hung from the Qur'anic verses and prophetic supplications or similar good supplications. Scholars differed about this type. Some of them permitted it and said: it is of the same kind as permissible incantations. Some of the scholars forbade it and said: it is forbidden and they argued for that with two arguments:

The first: the generality of the hadiths in prohibiting amulets and admonishing against them and ruling that they are polytheism, so it is not permissible to single out any amulet for permissibility except with a legal evidence that indicates that and there is nothing that indicates the specification. As for incantations, the authentic hadiths indicated that what is of them with Qur'anic verses and permissible supplications is permissible if it is in a language whose meaning is known and the one being treated does not rely on it, but rather believes that it is one of the means, because the Prophet, may God bless him and grant him peace, said: There is nothing wrong with incantations as long as they are not polytheism. And the Prophet, may God bless him and grant him peace, performed incantations and some of his companions performed incantations, and he said: There is no incantation except for the evil eye or a sting, and there are many hadiths on that. As for amulets, nothing was mentioned in any of the hadiths that made an exception for any of them, so it is obligatory to prohibit all of them in accordance with the general evidence.

The second argument: Blocking the means to polytheism. This is a great matter in Islamic law. It is known that if we permit amulets made from Qur’anic verses and permissible supplications, the door to polytheism will be opened, and the permissible amulet will be confused with the prohibited one, and it will be impossible to distinguish between them except with great difficulty. Therefore, it is necessary to block the door and close this path that leads to polytheism. This statement is correct because of the clarity of its evidence, and God is the One who grants success.

Source

Published in the Islamic University of Madinah Journal, Issue No. 4, Year 6, Rabi’ al-Thani 1394 AH, pp. 175-182.

The ruling of taking revenge.

by Sheikh Muhammad ibn Saleh Al-Uthaymeen • 3 min read • Fatawa

Reading can feel wonderfully passive. You sit down, open a book, and allow another person's carefully arranged thoughts to move through your head.

But something changes when you begin writing while you read. The page stops being a destination and becomes a conversation.

Questioner :

This is a message we received. It reads: "I, citizen S.D.A., from one of the Arab Muslim countries, submit these questions to His Eminence the Sheikh. His question is: Is revenge forbidden or permissible?"

The Sheikh :

Revenge?

Questioner :

Revenge, yes.

The Sheikh :

Taking revenge without transgression is permissible; meaning it means that you should repay the one who wronged you with the same wrongdoing, as God Almighty says: “And whoever avenges himself after being wronged - those are not to be blamed ” and He says: “So whoever transgresses against you, then transgress against him in the same way that he transgressed against you .” But if there is good in forgiveness, then it is better, as God Almighty says: “And to forgive is nearer to righteousness” and He says: “And if you punish, then punish with the like of that with which you were harmed. But if you are patient, it is better for those who are patient.” But on the condition that there is good and reform in forgiveness. If there is no good and reform in it, such as if the aggressor is a person known for evil and injustice, then forgiving him here is not appropriate. Rather, taking him with his punishment is better, because that deters him and deters those like him. If in this case, taking him with his punishment is not obligatory, and it is forbidden to forgive him because of that, if he is forgiven, he will continue to transgress and attack people

A response to the claim that Islam is harsh on women.

by Sheikh Abdul-Azeez ibn Baaz • 10 min read • Question and Answer

Question :

Our sister asks and says: Why are the rulings in Islam strict regarding women, yet most of the inhabitants of Hell are women?

Answer :

The rulings are not strict regarding her, and where is the strictness? Rather, there is leniency in some things. A woman is lenient in some things. If she menstruates, she does not pray, nor does she fast until she is purified. And if she is purified, she is only required to fast, and prayer is waived for her. And the 'aqiqah is only required one for her, and for a boy, two — in the 'aqiqah — where is the strictness?! ... There is leniency in it. Her blood money is fifty, this is from leniency and not from strictness. Her blood money is half the blood money of a man, half of fifty camels, this is from leniency and not from strictness.

She is also commanded to obey her husband and to be steadfast in obeying her Lord , and to stay in the house, and to stay away from what Allah has forbidden her. All of this is from Allah’s facilitation and ease for her. He did not burden her with things that would harm her and hurt her. He did not burden her with going out to work with men, spending on her husband. The husband is the one who spends on her. This is from her husband’s service to her, and from Allah’s honoring of her. Allah made it easy for her and did not make it difficult, but rather made it easy for her and honored her and made the husband the one who serves, works, toils, spends on her and takes care of her needs, and she takes care of the house and the children, and prepares things for the husband, such as food and other things.

In reality, she is both a servant and a slave. She serves her husband to the best of her ability and what Allah has prescribed for her, and the husband serves her with great effort. He goes and toils and works and takes risks to seek sustenance so that he can provide for her and her children.

As for the statement that women constitute the majority of the inhabitants of Hell, as the Prophet ﷺ said, he clarified this in his hadith:

“ O women! Give charity and seek forgiveness often, for I have seen that you are the majority of the inhabitants of Hell.” A woman asked, “Why, O Messenger of Allah?” He replied, “Because you frequently curse and are ungrateful to your husbands. If one of you were to treat him well for a lifetime, and then she saw something from him that upset her, she would say, ‘I have never seen any good from you at all.’ This is an expression of the majority, and this is something we observe and know, and people know it: that if a woman sees something from her husband that displeases her, she forgets his previous kindness and says, ‘You are the one who is like this, and I have never seen this from you, and I have never seen that from you,’ except for those whom Allah guides.”

Cursing and lying are common among them, as is cursing spouses, children, and people. That is why he said: They curse a lot , meaning: insults, curses, and bad words. Cursing is used for bad words even if it does not include: May Allah curse so-and-so, as Allah said in the Qur’an:

“ And the accursed tree in the Qur’an” [Al-Isra: 60]

It is condemned in the Qur’an. Allah condemned it and criticized it, saying:

“Indeed, the tree of Zaqqum is the food of the sinful. Like molten metal” [Ad-Dukhan: 45].

So He condemned it, and this is called cursing. So, condemning something and cursing it with words that are not good, such as: May Allah disgrace him, such as: There is no good in him, such as: So-and-so is stingy, so-and-so is a coward, all of this is called cursing, even if he did not say: May Allah curse him, it is still called cursing. The point is that they curse a lot, meaning they speak a lot of bad words and cause harm to their husbands and children, and perhaps they do this to relatives and neighbors. This is mostly due to ignorance, lack of religion, and weakness, but there are good and virtuous qualities in them…

Source:

Audio Fatwa Sheikh Ibn Baz (Official Site: binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/18529). Sahih Bukhari (No. 3241) Sahih Bukhari (No.304)
